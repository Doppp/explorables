# Start with the words

You are expected to know basic programming, arrays, and terminal use. You are **not** expected to
already know AI or machine-learning terminology. An unfamiliar word is a missing prerequisite for
the current step, not evidence that you are bad at the subject.

## Words for this step

A **definition** explains what a word means in this course. An **example** shows one case that fits
the definition. A **non-example** shows a nearby case that does not fit. A **prediction** is a
starting guess about what will happen after the setup is clear; it is not a grade.

For example, a programming function is a named piece of code with a responsibility. A variable is
not a function, even though a function may use variables. The example and non-example make the
boundary clearer than the name alone.

## Recognition before prediction

The first question after a new definition should check that the words are usable. Only then should
the course ask you to predict a result, manipulate an explorable, or explain a mechanism.

If a term remains unclear, stop and ask your tutor: “Explain *that term* with an example and a
non-example.” The tutor should repair that one prerequisite before returning to the checkpoint.

## Check your understanding

In your own words, explain why “I do not know this term yet” should pause the checkpoint rather than
count as an incorrect prediction.

## Recap

- AI vocabulary is taught, not assumed.
- Recognition comes before prediction.
- Predictions are useful starting guesses and are not grades.
- The [course glossary](../GLOSSARY.md) is available for review, but each lesson still defines the
  words it needs.
